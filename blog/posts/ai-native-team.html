Becoming an AI-Native Team: Every Team Needs Its Own Memory

Draft

Every team needs its own memory, and cross-team work needs a shared one that a legal agent clears.

My personal notes live in Obsidian as plain markdown: what I read, what I learn, what I decide and why. An agent reads and writes that layer with me. It's close to what Andrej Karpathy calls an LLM wiki, a markdown knowledge base that an agent keeps current. When I need the reasoning behind an old decision, I ask, and the agent comes back with it, linked to where it came from.

That works for one person. Now picture asking your team's agent why a pilot got killed last quarter. It can't tell you, and a better model wouldn't help. The reason lives in someone's head, or in a chat thread nobody can find. The team has no memory.

The context foundation

We solved this once already, for data. Nobody runs shared analytics on CSVs sitting in someone's inbox, so we built a data foundation. Agents need the same thing one layer up, for why decisions were made and how the work gets done. I call it the context foundation.

Why this time is different

"We tried knowledge management. We have the wiki nobody updates to prove it."

Fair. Wikis died because people had to write them and people had to browse them, and neither side got much back. Now the main reader is an agent that pulls up the same note again and again, so every note is worth more. The agent can also do the upkeep, the thing that killed every wiki, because markdown is plain text it can read, write, and diff. People approve; the agent maintains.

A layer for every team

The obvious move is one context layer for the whole company. In pharma that's the wrong shape. The walls between commercial, medical, and R&D are legal requirements, and an agent with total recall is also an agent with total gossip.

My view: every team gets its own layer, cross-team work gets a shared one, and the company sets the rules they all follow.

TeamTeam layers

Each team owns its decisions and how its work gets done, readable on a need-to-know basis. Tie the layer to a function, like forecasting or market access, so it survives the next reorg.

SharedCross-team memory

Cross-team projects, like a launch, need one place where the whole decision lives. Legal writes the crossing rules once. A legal agent checks every record against them, clears the routine ones, and sends anything unclear to a person in Legal, who also samples what the agent cleared.

CompanyCommon rules

One set of controls for every layer: the record format, trust tiers, permissions, audit, retention, and legal holds.

The agent reads with your permissions: your team's layer, plus whatever shared records you're cleared for. A decision moves outward in steps:

Start here: the ten-decision test

Before you build anything, agree with Legal, Privacy, and IT on what may be captured, and keep legal, HR, and other sensitive meetings out. Then get a baseline. Pick ten decisions your team made last quarter, ask your agent (or a new hire) why each one was made, and count how many answers come back with a source. That's your score.
