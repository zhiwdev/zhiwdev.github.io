# Zhi's writing voice

Reference for anyone (human or agent) writing or editing posts on this blog. Use it as the
writing sample the `humanizer` skill matches. Examples are lines Zhi wrote.

## Who is talking

A practitioner who leads analytics and AI teams in pharma and builds things on the side.
First person, confident, practical. Writes from what was done or would be done, not from
theory. Opinions are stated plainly and owned ("My rule:", "My bet is").

## Sentences

- Mostly short and medium declaratives. Mix in a longer sentence when an idea needs room.
- Contractions are normal: it's, don't, I'd, here's.
- Plain words over formal ones. "Killed", "dies", "costs an hour", "pipes and permissions".
- Semicolons join two balanced halves:
  > Killing a bad idea at the demo stage costs an hour; killing it after implementation costs a week.
- Colon-led rules and answers:
  > My rule: if I can't explain the technical proposal back in my own words, it's not ready for implementation.
- Dashes are allowed but sparing, about one every few paragraphs, for a real aside. Never as the
  default connector.

## Moves Zhi uses

- Opens with a concrete scene or setup, then widens to the general point.
- Names things memorably, once: "context foundation", "brilliant amnesiac",
  "total recall is also total gossip", "honest is what ships".
- Quotes the skeptic's objection directly, then answers it:
  > "We tried knowledge management. We have the wiki nobody updates to prove it."
- Practical structure: numbered steps, week-by-week plans, checklists, a short test ("the Tuesday test").
- Admits limits and costs out loud (the labor bill, "one project, no controlled comparison").
- Bold only for a coined term the first time it appears, or for a short run of labeled paragraphs
  (**Governance.**, **Access.**).

## Keep out

- More than one "not X, it's Y" contrast per section. Keep it only when it corrects something readers
  actually believe ("a retrieval and workflow pattern, not a model-building project").
- One-line closers that restate the paragraph, and rows of dramatic fragments.
- Run-ups: "Let's be honest", "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "First, a clarification".
- Stock AI words: pivotal, crucial, delve, landscape, robust (figurative), quietly, showcase.
- Invented facts, numbers, feelings, or anecdotes. If a detail is missing, ask Zhi.

## Guardrails (pharma employee writing in public)

- Nothing about any employer's work, systems, data, vendors, programs, or plans, including
  anonymized versions ("at my company we…").
- No commentary on specific drugs or brands, Zhi's employer's or competitors'.
- No vendor endorsements. Tools Zhi uses personally can be named neutrally.
- Examples use synthetic or generic data. Named colleagues become generic ("the person who was there").
- Posts end with a views-my-own disclaimer.
